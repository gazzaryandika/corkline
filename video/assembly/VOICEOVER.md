# Corkline video: voiceover script

One line per section, in the order of the rough cut (`assembly/corkline-rough-cut.mp4`). Times are where each
section starts in that cut; the length in brackets is how long its picture runs. At a calm pace a voice reads about
**2.5 words a second**, so the word count after each line tells you whether it fits (aim to stay under).

Tips for the voice tool (ElevenLabs, CapCut text-to-speech, …):
- Generate **one audio file per section**, so you can slide each one to its section start in the editor.
- A warm, unhurried voice. Leave a short breath at each section start; the picture changes there.
- Waiting time may be cut: if a line runs long, trim the picture's holds rather than speeding the voice up.

## Motivation (~30 s)

**Title slide** (6.5 s): *no voice; music only, or:*
> Corkline. *(1 word)*

**Intro 1: scattered** (10 s)
> Have you ever had this problem? Interviews, sticky notes, PDFs, photos, spreadsheets. Your design material ends up scattered everywhere. *(19 words)*

**Intro 2: only in your head** (8 s)
> The organisation lives only in your head, and the connections between it all are hard to see. *(17 words)*

**Intro 2b: distill** (5.5 s)
> Distilling all that research, and finding what connects, is the hard part. *(12 words)*

**Intro 2c: introducing** (3 s)
> Introducing Corkline. *(2 words)*

**Intro 3: pulling it together** (8 s)
> A reasoning board for design projects. It puts everything on one board, and makes your reasoning visible. *(17 words)*

## Demonstration (~2 min)

**Demo 1: new project** (6 s)
> Start a project: give it a name, and describe the challenge in a sentence. *(14 words)*

**Demo 2: add the brief** (8.5 s)
> Then add your documents. Here, the project brief. Corkline's AI reads it and drafts cards. *(15 words)*

**Demo 3: review the cards** (9.8 s)
> Nothing reaches the board until you accept it. Each draft keeps the quote it came from. This one only lists methods, so we reject it. *(24 words)*

**Demo 4: link by hand** (5.5 s)
> Drag from card to card to link them. *(8 words)*

**Demo 5: your own card** (12.9 s)
> You can add your own cards too. This assumption is ours: kids learn about money by doing, not by being told. The board flags it still needs a problem behind it, so we link one. *(35 words)*

**Demo 6: an interview** (22.3 s)
> Next, an interview transcript, here a sample one. Corkline looks for evidence: what people actually said. We accept the quotes, and link them to our assumption. The warnings clear as the chain fills in. The cards we haven't linked keep their flag, so nothing slips through.

**Demo 7: the board grows** (10 s)
> A few weeks in, a board might look like this: reasoning traced from problem to prototype, so every idea leads back to its evidence.
> *(These cards are illustrative. Keep "a board might look like this" in mind if you rephrase; don't present them as real results.)*

**Demo 8: the AI Assistant** (11.5 s)
> The AI Assistant is a second pair of eyes on your reasoning. It answers, checks and suggests, but it never invents ideas. You decide. *(24 words)*

**Demo 9: Find connections** (15 s)
> Find connections looks for missing links, starting with the gaps. Every suggestion comes with a reason. You accept or reject it, and the gaps close.

## Reflection (35 s, timed to the voice)

**R0: the goal** (0.75–8.2 s)
> We set out to make design reasoning visible. Does Corkline do that? Mostly, yes.

**R1: what worked** (8.2–14.8 s)
> The AI's role is small and easy to check, and it caught twelve skipped steps on our own board.

**R2: limits** (14.8–21.6 s)
> But we only tested it on two projects, by ourselves, and real projects loop back.

**R3: garbage in, garbage out** (21.6–29.9 s)
> AI has grown immensely, even in the last few months. But garbage in, garbage out still stands.

**R4: closing** (29.9–35 s)
> Our most useful decisions were about what the AI should not do.
