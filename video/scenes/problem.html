PDFdesk_research.pdf · Reddit, 121 threads

3.2 Method. Threads from r/Parenting, r/personalfinance and r/Netherlands were collected with an Apify scraper and clustered with assistance from Claude, then coded by hand for recurring concerns.

3.3 Findings. Parents describe cash as the default teaching tool for value: counting coins, paying at the till, physically handing money over. Several parents note that card and phone payments happen “out of sight” of children.

The most frequent concern, across 38 threads, is that parents worry kids can't count, spend or plan once cash is gone.

A smaller group (11 threads) reports using prepaid kids' cards, but describes the apps as built for parents, not for children, with little that a six-year-old can follow on their own.

3.4 Limitations. Reddit skews towards English-speaking, higher-income parents; findings are indicative and should be compared with the Dutch interview data before being generalised.

3.5 Competitors. GoHenry, Gimi and Greenlight combine pocket money with lessons; none foregrounds sustainability or the impact of where money goes.
